Price List and Receipts

The administrator decides what things cost. The finance desk hands the patient a piece of paper proving they paid it. Neither one works without the other.

From clinic demos, 3 Sep 2026 6 requirements 5 decisions outstanding Companion to “Finance Desk” and “Walk-in Diagnostics”

Why these are one requirement

A receipt is only as good as the prices on it. If the price list is a number somebody typed from memory, the receipt is a piece of paper with a guess printed on it. The administrator sets the prices once, at onboarding; every bill and every receipt afterwards is those numbers being read back.

They also fail together. A clinic that cannot print a receipt cannot prove a patient paid — and a patient who paid ₹500 for an X-ray with nothing in their hand has no way to show the department they are owed one.

Missing: any way to list services — there is only an upsert — any screen at all for prices, and any printed receipt. The receipt is a known gap, recorded in the handover doc as “invoices are numbered and complete; nothing prints one yet.”

Warning for whoever picks this up

Invoice lines deliberately copy the service name and unit price at the moment of billing rather than pointing at the service row. This looks like duplication and is not: it is what lets a receipt reprinted in 2029 show the ₹500 that was actually charged, not the ₹800 the X-ray costs by then. Do not “normalise” it.

The price list

FR-PRC-01

An administrator sets what everything costs

New screen

The hospital administrator opens a screen and sees everything the clinic charges for, with its price, and can change it.

FieldNotes
NameWhat it is called on the bill — “Chest X-ray”, “CBC”
CategoryConsultation, diagnostic, procedure — so the list stays legible
DepartmentWhich department performs it, where that applies
PriceEntered in rupees, stored in paise
Tax rateZero for most clinics — see decision 3
ActiveSwitched off without being deleted

The screen shows the whole list at once, grouped and searchable. An administrator pricing a clinic is comparing forty numbers against each other, not editing one in isolation.

For the developer

The model and the upsert exist. What is missing is a list endpoint and the screen. Prices are integer paise in the database and rupees on screen — convert at the edge, never store a float.

FR-PRC-02

Prices are set when the clinic is onboarded

Behaviour exists

Pricing is part of onboarding, not something a clinic discovers is empty on its first morning. Whoever onboards sits with the clinic and enters their charges.

  • A clinic with no prices raises no invoices, and its check-in gate stays open. This is already how the system behaves, and it is right — a clinic can start using the software before its pricing is settled.
  • But an empty price list should say so plainly on the finance desk rather than looking like a bug: “No services are priced yet. Ask your administrator.”
FR-PRC-03

Changing a price later

Model already correct

Prices go up. When they do, every invoice and receipt already issued keeps the price it was issued at.

  • A change applies to bills raised from that moment on.
  • Nothing already billed changes, ever — not the invoice, not a reprint of its receipt, not the collection figures for a past date.
  • A service no longer offered is switched off, not deleted. Reporting groups by service, and a deleted one makes last month's report unexplainable.
Scope

Because invoice lines already copy the price, this is mostly satisfied by the data model. The work is making sure the screens don't undo it, and that the price-change screen says what it does.

The receipt

FR-RCP-01

Print a receipt for every payment

New build

Every time the finance desk takes money, they can print a receipt and hand it over. It is the patient's proof of payment — and in the walk-in test flow, it is what they carry to the department.

  • Available immediately after taking the payment, and again later from the invoice.
  • Prints on the paper the clinic already owns — A5 or thermal, see decision 4.
  • Carries the clinic's own name and logo, as the prescription already does.

  • Top — who and where. Clinic name, logo, address, phone. Receipt number and date-time. Patient name, patient ID, age, sex, mobile. The doctor, where there is one — a walk-in test has none and the receipt should not pretend otherwise.
  • Middle — what they are paying for. One line per item, showing the rate, the quantity and the amount.
  • Bottom — the money, in this order and never collapsed: total charged; discount with the promotion named; payable; paid now and how; balance outstanding.
  • Who took it — the name of the person at the desk, and space for the clinic's stamp.
FR-RCP-03

A receipt is for a payment, not for a bill

Gets got wrong

This is the part most likely to be built wrong, so it is stated on its own.

  • One invoice can have several receipts. A ₹1,100 bill where the patient pays ₹500 now and ₹600 on the way out is one invoice and two receipts — each showing what was paid at that moment and what remained. A receipt that reprints the whole bill every time is a receipt the patient can use to claim they paid twice.
  • Reprints are marked. The first print is the receipt; every print after it says DUPLICATE on its face.
  • Every print is recorded — which receipt, by whom, when. Cheap to store, and it is the answer when somebody asks how a second copy exists.

Why this one matters

Two identical unmarked receipts for one payment is how cash goes missing in a clinic — and the person it exposes is the finance desk, who has no way to prove which copy was the original.

Decisions still needed

1. Who owns the prices — the platform, or the clinic's administrator?

The requirement says both: set at onboarding, changed by the clinic afterwards.

Recommendation The clinic's administrator owns it, and onboarding is simply the first time they use the screen. Prices change far too often for a clinic to have to ask you.

2. Does the per-doctor price override belong on this screen?

The model already supports Dr A's consultation costing more than Dr B's. Recommendation On the doctor's own setup screen, where the rest of that doctor's arrangements already live — not mixed into the price list.

3. GST — what must a receipt legally carry?

Most clinical services in India are GST-exempt, and the tax field exists but is zero. If any of your clinics are GST-registered, their receipt legally needs more than what is listed above — GSTIN, HSN or SAC codes, a tax breakdown, a specific document title. That is a compliance question, not a design one, and it needs answering before receipts print. If none are, say so and the field stays zero.

4. What paper does it print on?

A5 from a normal printer, or a thermal roll from a till printer? Different layouts, and the clinic already owns one of them. Ask the ortho clinic what is on their desk. One question now, or a rebuild later.

5. Is the receipt number the invoice number, or its own sequence?

Recommendation Its own sequence. One invoice can produce several receipts, so they cannot share a number without a suffix — and a receipt with a suffix invites somebody to invent one.

Rules that apply throughout
