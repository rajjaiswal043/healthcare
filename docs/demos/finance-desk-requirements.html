Finance Desk and Promotions

The clinics we demoed to want money handled before the front desk, by somebody whose whole job is money — and they want to be able to give a patient a discount without it being a hand-written note.

From clinic demos, 3 Sep 2026 7 requirements 6 decisions outstanding

What is changing

Today the front desk does everything at the door: finds the patient, registers them, books, takes the money, checks them in. These clinics split that job in two. A finance person now meets the patient first — finds or registers them, settles which doctor they are seeing, raises the bill, applies a discount if one has been authorised, and takes payment. The patient reaches the front desk only once the money is settled, and the front desk's job shrinks to arrival and the queue.

The shape of the work

This changes who does what, not how money works. Invoices, payments, receipts and the pay-first rule already exist and are unchanged. What is genuinely new is promotions. Everything else is a role, a screen that puts existing steps in one flow, and a reporting view.

Requirements

FR-FIN-01

A finance role

Exists · configure

A member of staff whose entire job is money. They can find and register patients and handle everything financial, and they can do nothing clinical and nothing to the diary.

Can doWhy
Search patients, open a recordThe first thing they do when somebody walks in
Register a new patientA first-time patient must not be sent to another desk
See which doctors are consulting today, and whether they have roomTo answer “can I be seen today?” before taking money
Open today's visit for a doctorSo the bill has something to attach to — see decision 1
Raise an invoice, add and remove chargesThe bill itself
Apply an authorised promotionFR-PRO-02
Take payment, print a receiptThe money
See all financial reportingFR-FIN-03

Cannot do: book or reschedule a future appointment, cancel one, order a test, open or write a consultation, prescribe, record vitals. Refunds, waivers and deferrals stay with the administrator — that is already the rule and should not be relaxed for this role.

For the developer

Roles are already editable per clinic at Settings → Roles and permissions; the catalogue is packages/db/src/permission-catalogue.ts. If Finance becomes a seventh built-in role, it needs two changes that must not drift: an entry in SYSTEM_ROLES and a backfill migration for clinics that already exist. These have drifted once before and caused a bug. Migrations run under FORCE row-level security, so a backfill must loop tenants with set_config('app.tenant_id', …) or it matches zero rows and reports success.

FR-FIN-02

The finance desk — one screen, one flow

New build

Everything the finance person does for a walk-in patient, in one place, in the order it actually happens — so they are not moving between four screens with a patient standing in front of them.

  1. Find the patient. By name, mobile or UHID, with enough detail shown to tell two people of the same name apart.
  2. Or register them. Without leaving the screen, with the same duplicate checking the front desk uses today.
  3. Choose the doctor. Who is consulting today, and whether they still have room.
  4. Raise the bill. The doctor's consultation fee comes in automatically; other priced services can be added.
  5. Apply a promotion, if one has been authorised.
  6. Take the payment. Cash, UPI or card, as today.
  7. Print the receipt, and send the patient to the front desk.
Acceptance

A new patient can be found-or-registered, billed and paid in a single screen without navigating away, and arrives at the front desk with nothing outstanding. Steps 1, 2, 4, 6 and 7 already exist as endpoints — this is a screen over work that is done.

FR-FIN-03

Finance reporting

Extends existing

The finance person owns the numbers, and the owner asks them — not the doctor — where the day's money went. For a chosen day or date range:

ReportContent
CollectionsTotal taken, split by cash / UPI / card
By doctorPatients seen, gross billed, discount given, net collected
DiscountsEvery promotion applied: which, how much, by whom, authorised by whom
OutstandingBills raised and unpaid, and anything deferred
Refunds and reversalsMoney returned to a patient, kept separate from entries recorded in error
The rule that decides the design

Gross billed, discount and net collected are three separate figures, never one. A ₹600 consultation given away free and recorded as a ₹0 bill leaves the clinic unable to say how much it gave away, and unable to pay a doctor on what they billed. Recorded as ₹600 billed less ₹600 discount, both questions have answers.

FR-PRO-01

The administrator sets up promotions

New build

Clinics give discounts for real reasons — a doctor's own patient, a camp, a staff family member, a hardship case. Today that is a verbal instruction and a hand-written note. The administrator should set the discounts up in advance so the finance person applies a known thing rather than inventing one.

FieldNotes
CodeShort, typed or picked at billing — CAMP50
NameWhat it is for, in words. Read by whoever approves it later
TypePercentage, or flat rupee amount
Value1–100 for a percentage; a rupee amount for a flat discount
Maximum discountOptional cap, so “50% off” cannot become ₹4,000
Valid from / toA camp offer stops working when the camp ends
ActiveSwitched off without being deleted
  • 100% is allowed. It is the “don't charge this patient” case, and the reason the feature exists.
  • A flat discount larger than the bill reduces it to zero and never below — the clinic does not owe money because a promo was too big.
  • A promotion that has ever been used cannot be deleted or edited, only switched off. An invoice from March has to keep explaining itself in September.
  • Only the administrator creates, changes or switches off a promotion. Finance applies them and cannot invent one.
FR-PRO-02

Applying a promotion at billing

New build

The administrator rings the finance desk: “this one is my patient, give them 50%.” The finance person applies it and takes the reduced amount.

  • The discount is a line on the invoice, not a changed price. The doctor's fee still reads ₹600 and the discount reads −₹300. A bill that simply says ₹300 loses the fact that anything was given away.
  • Who authorised it is recorded, and is required. The instruction arrives by phone, so the system cannot see it — the finance person names the approver from the clinic's own staff list. Free text is not enough.
  • A promotion can only be applied before payment is taken. Afterwards it is a refund or a correction — an administrator's job, and already built.
  • Applying one is recorded permanently: which promotion, the amount, who applied it, who authorised it, when.
Why the approver is named

Without it, the record shows the finance person gave away money and shows nothing about the instruction they were following. That is how discount schemes go wrong in clinics, and the person most exposed by it is the finance person. Naming the approver protects them.

FR-FIN-04

The front desk after this change

Exists · configure

Once finance takes the money, the front desk checks the patient in and runs the queue. Billing is no longer its job.

For the developer

Do not remove billing from the front desk in code. Some clinics keep one person doing both, and the roles screen lets each clinic decide: a clinic adopting this flow removes invoice:write and payment:collect from its own Front desk role. Configuration, not a code change — it is the reason that screen was built.

The pay-first rule already enforces the sequence. Check-in refuses to put a patient in the queue while money is outstanding, and that lives in the API, not the UI — so a patient who skips finance is stopped at the front desk automatically. Nothing new is needed for it.

Decisions still needed

These change what gets built, and are far cheaper to answer now than to discover halfway through.

1. What does the bill attach to?

An invoice hangs off a visit today. If finance bills before anyone has booked, either the bill floats and is matched later, or the act of billing opens the visit.

Recommendation Billing opens today's visit. Finance picks the doctor anyway, the walk-in visit already exists, and the invoice stays attached to something real. Note this is not the same as letting finance book — opening today's visit and booking Thursday afternoon are different powers.

2. Are doctors paid on gross or net?

A ₹600 consultation given away free: does the doctor's figure show ₹600 or ₹0? There is no right answer — it is a commercial decision, and it must be settled before the reports are built, because it decides what they add up.

3. Can finance apply a promotion with nobody named?

Recommendation No. Naming the approver is the control. If the answer is yes, say so explicitly — it is a deliberate decision to give the finance desk unsupervised discretion over price.

4. Are promo codes told to patients, or internal only?

If patients are ever given a code, codes need to be hard to guess and usage limits start to matter. If purely internal, CAMP50 is fine. This changes the design.

5. Do promotions apply to tests, or only the consultation fee?

An X-ray and a consultation are both priced services. “50% off” needs to say off what.

6. Does finance close a drawer at the end of the day?

Not raised in the demo, and usually the next thing asked for once one person owns the cash. Worth confirming it is out of scope rather than assumed in.

Rules the new work must not break

Money is integer paise everywhere — never float, never decimal rupees. Payments are append-only rows, never edited or deleted; a correction is a new compensating row. Every clinic's data is isolated by row-level security, so every new table needs its tenant policy — pnpm rls:check fails the build if it is missing.
